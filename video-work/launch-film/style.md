# Style direction — launch-film

**Style from the roster:** Launch Film (the house flagship register).
**Kind: narrative** → story-spine check.

**A/B pick (stage-4 gate, 2026-07-28): FRAME A — gallery-dark triptych.** Jason's call;
the look critic's recommendation held. Why: canon fidelity (the Launch Film canon's warm
textured dark field with floating framed windows, dark/light value labeling), the exhibits
own all the color in the room, and the house recedes to frame them — which is this film's
stated job. Frame B (light editorial stack) was the honest alternative but re-pointed the
World constant to a light field and reduced the exhibits to citations. Build notes carried
from the review: proper sentence case on the bottom mono caption ("A 15-minute brand
interview, then every video ships in your look"), and map the serif off the Georgia
fallback to a distinctive editorial serif HyperFrames renders reliably (Playfair Display,
snapshot-verified before the full render).

**Why this style, against the canon deck.** The canon anchor for Launch Film is the Claude
Code in-app browser launch teardown (`references/canon-deck.md` §1): a full narrative arc
carried by an eased camera on a warm textured field, floating rounded windows that never go
fullscreen, dark/light doing the labeling, a wordmark resolve, silent but built to carry
mood. This film borrows that register exactly. Our warm charcoal wall is the canon's warm
textured field; the three exhibits are the canon's floating windows, framed and never
fullscreen; the camera narrates by punching into the exhibit being named right now (canon
transferable rule 7, "camera is narration"); the piece resolves on the wordmark plus the
install line. A Kinetic Essay was the alternative (this is an argument), but the argument
here is carried by SHOWING three rendered brands, not by kinetic type alone, so the Launch
Film's reveal-and-proof spine fits and the essay's text-only spine does not.

**Signature moves (3 chosen):**
1. **The gallery frame.** The three exhibit videos play as framed clips — real video
   elements inside the composition (HyperFrames framework-owned media playback) — hung as
   floating rounded windows on the warm charcoal wall. The house holds no saturated hue, so
   the exhibits' own colors (Ledgerline green, Steepwell amber, Fern green) are the only
   color in the room. The frame recedes; the work reads as the subject.
2. **Eased hero push-in per exhibit.** One camera move per evidence beat — punch into the
   exhibit being named, hold at its dwell so the placard reads (~2.5s), release. Never two
   moves in a beat, never a yo-yo. The move is the narration.
3. **One bone word carries the turn.** The accent is value, not hue: the single brightest
   bone word per frame lands the point (the turn word "decision", the thesis, the install
   line). Everything else steps down to muted warm gray. One bone accent per frame, strict.

**Variant axes chosen (Launch Film archetype):**
- **Pace:** measured slow-burn (gallery tempo), not punchy.
- **Voice:** type-led (silent), not VO-led.
- **Reveal style:** cut-to each exhibit (hard cuts between framed works), not build-on.
- **Closing-frame world:** primary dark — the house has one world (the charcoal wall), so
  the resolve lands in it rather than a second emphasis frame.

**Ledger note.** `style-ledger.md` has no rows yet — this is the brand's first video, so
there is nothing to vary against. Recorded here so video 2 rotates the axes against this
row (next Launch Film should move off slow-burn/type-led/cut-to on at least one axis).

**One ease system.** A single eased push-in/settle (slow-in, slow-out) declared once and
reused for every exhibit reveal and every type entrance. Staggered entrances, nothing
arrives all at once, revealed lines hold ~2.5s.

**Accent/wordmark resolution.** The wordmark `video-direction` is bone (the house has no
accent hue), so it does not compete with a second colored accent — no logo-exemption path.
The only rule to hold: on the resolve frame, the install line is the single bone accent and
the wordmark sits in the same bone family without a second brighter element stealing it.
